About

We study how climate extremes affect earthen infrastructure and the communities it serves. Our research at Tufts University combines soil mechanics, infrastructure resilience, and environmental justice.

Challenge

Drought, flooding, and wildfire change the moisture and temperature conditions that influence soil behavior. These changes can affect the stability of slopes, dams, and levees. Compound and cascading hazards add to this risk, with consequences for the communities that depend on these systems.

Contribution

We investigate the resilience and adaptation of critical infrastructure to extreme events in a changing climate. Our work connects geotechnical engineering with environmental justice, with particular attention to risks facing disadvantaged communities.

Approach

We use analytical and numerical methods to study water flow, heat transfer, and stress in variably saturated soils. We apply this understanding to assess slopes, dams, and levees under changing climate conditions. Our research draws on climate science, hydrology, geosciences, social science, remote sensing, and data science.

Principal investigator

Farshid Vahedifard is Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Tufts and the Louis Berger Chair. His research examines how climate extremes affect infrastructure and disadvantaged communities. Read his full profile at Tufts CEE · facultyprofiles.tufts.edu.
