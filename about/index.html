About

The Climate Resilient and Equitable Infrastructure Lab, in the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Tufts University, studies how climate extremes affect earthen infrastructure and the communities it serves.

Challenge

Drought, flooding, and wildfire change soil moisture and temperature, and with them soil strength and deformation. These changes can reduce the stability of slopes, dams, and levees. Compound and cascading hazards, in which events overlap or follow one another, add to this risk. When these structures fail, the consequences reach the communities that depend on them.

Contribution

Our work focuses on critical infrastructure such as levees and dams: how it withstands extreme events in a changing climate, and how it can be adapted. We pair geotechnical engineering with environmental justice, paying particular attention to the risks facing disadvantaged communities.

Approach

We use analytical and numerical methods to study water flow, heat transfer, and stress in variably saturated soils. We apply these models to slopes, dams, and levees under changing climate conditions. Much of this work is collaborative, drawing on climate science, hydrology, geosciences, social science, remote sensing, and data science.

Principal investigator
