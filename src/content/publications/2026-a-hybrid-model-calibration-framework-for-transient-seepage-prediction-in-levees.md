abstract: "Accurate prediction of transient pore-water pressure (PWP) is essential for reliable assessment of levee performance and decision-making, as seepage-driven processes directly influence failure mechanisms such as slope instability, uplift, and internal erosion. Physics-based numerical seepage models are widely used in engineering practice, but their predictive accuracy is often limited by model parameter uncertainty, simplified boundary conditions, and unmodeled processes, particularly under transient hydrologic forcing. These limitations introduce uncertainty in evaluating levee performance under extreme events. This study develops a hybrid modeling framework that enhances predictive accuracy while preserving the physical consistency of numerical seepage models. The framework integrates transient finite-element (FE) seepage modeling, sensitivity-guided parameter calibration, and a data-driven residual-learning layer based on a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. The LSTM is trained exclusively to learn structured residual errors between FE predictions and field measurements and operates as an additive correction without modifying governing equations. Application to a field-instrumented levee demonstrates that while calibration improves overall agreement, systematic discrepancies persist under transient conditions. The residual-learning layer reduces prediction error by approximately 70%, lowering the RMSE from about 11 cm to approximately 3. Interpretability analysis using TimeSHAP confirms that residual corrections are governed primarily by physically meaningful drivers, including hydrologic forcing and recent system state. By reducing structured model discrepancy, the proposed framework improves transient seepage predictions while retaining a physics-based predictive baseline and providing transparency into the factors governing residual correction. The proposed framework provides a foundation for future sensor-integrated digital-twin applications as additional validation data become available."
